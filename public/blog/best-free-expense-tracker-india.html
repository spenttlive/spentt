What to look for in a free expense tracker

Before comparing apps, it's worth being clear about what "free" actually means. A truly free expense tracker should have:

  • No subscription fee — core features available without paying
  • No mandatory bank linking — you should be able to use it without sharing banking credentials
  • No data selling — your spending patterns shouldn't be monetised through advertising
  • Data portability — you should be able to export or delete your data
  • Works on your phone — Indian users primarily use Android and iPhone, not desktop

Why most Indians should avoid bank-linking expense trackers

Several popular expense trackers in India — including ET Money, Fi Money, and the now-defunct Walnut — work by reading your SMS messages to automatically import transactions. While convenient, this approach has serious privacy implications:

  • The app gains access to all your financial SMS, not just bank transactions
  • Your complete spending history is stored on the company's servers
  • If the company is acquired or shuts down, your data goes with it
  • Your spending patterns can be used to push financial products

After Walnut was acquired by SBI Cards and effectively shut down, thousands of users lost access to years of spending history. This is a risk that's easy to avoid by choosing an app that stores data in your own account — like Spentt does with Google Drive.

Manual vs automatic expense tracking — which is better?

Automatic tracking (via SMS or bank import) is more convenient but less effective at building financial awareness. When your bank automatically imports a ₹450 Zomato order, you never consciously engage with that transaction.

Manual tracking — logging each expense yourself in 5 seconds — forces a brief moment of awareness at the point of spending. Research consistently shows that people who log expenses manually spend 15-20% less than people who rely on automatic imports, simply because the act of logging creates conscious engagement.

The best expense tracker isn't the one that does the most automatically — it's the one you actually use every day. And the one you'll use every day is the one with the least friction.
