Excel vs App for Expense Tracking — Which Is Actually Better?

By Spentt August 2026 8 min read

If you track your expenses at all, you probably started with a spreadsheet. Excel or Google Sheets, a simple table with dates and amounts, maybe some category columns — functional, flexible, and completely under your control.

Then you downloaded an expense tracker app. Maybe you stuck with it for a week. Maybe you're still using it. Or maybe you went back to the spreadsheet, or gave up entirely.

The Excel vs app debate isn't as simple as "one is better." Both have real strengths. The right answer depends on how you actually spend money and when you actually do your tracking. This piece gives you an honest comparison so you can decide which approach — or which combination — will actually work for you.

The short answer: Excel is better for analysis and monthly reviews. Apps are better for logging in real time. The best outcome for most people is an app that gives you the data control and privacy of a spreadsheet — without the friction of opening a laptop every time you buy a coffee.

Why 38% of people still use spreadsheets

Research consistently shows that a significant portion of people who track expenses — roughly a third to half — still use Excel or Google Sheets rather than a dedicated app. This isn't ignorance of better tools. These are deliberate choices, and the reasons are legitimate.

The fundamental problem with spreadsheet expense tracking isn't the analysis — it's the logging. You're not near your laptop when you pay for a coffee at 8am, a lunch at 1pm, and a cab at 7pm. You tell yourself you'll enter it all at the end of the day. Then you do it from memory. Memory is unreliable. You miss things, round amounts, forget categories. Your data becomes an approximation rather than a record.

The best of both worlds

The ideal setup for most people isn't a binary choice. It's using the right tool for each job:

  • Daily logging: Mobile app — immediate, frictionless, accurate
  • Monthly deep analysis: Export to CSV and open in Excel for custom analysis
  • Data storage: An app that stores data in your own cloud storage (like Spentt's Google Drive approach) gives you both mobile convenience and spreadsheet-level data ownership

This is exactly how Spentt is designed. Log on your phone in 5 seconds. Your data is stored in your own Google Drive as a JSON file — which you can export to CSV at any time and open in Excel for any analysis you want. You get the convenience of an app and the data control of a spreadsheet.

Why manual logging beats automatic bank import

Both Excel and manual apps require you to log expenses yourself. Many people see this as a weakness compared to apps that automatically import from your bank. It isn't.

The act of logging — even typing five words and a number — creates a moment of conscious engagement with your spending that automatic import bypasses. Research consistently shows that people who log expenses manually spend 15-20% less than people who rely on automatic imports — not because they restrict themselves, but because the act of logging makes spending conscious.

When your bank auto-imports a ₹450 Zomato order, you don't engage with it. When you type "Zomato ₹450" yourself, you spend a half-second thinking about whether it was worth it. That half-second is where financial awareness lives.

Frequently asked questions

Is Excel good for tracking expenses?
Excel is good for monthly analysis, custom reporting, and people who reconcile transactions from bank statements at the end of each month. It's not good for daily real-time logging — the friction of opening a laptop makes consistent logging impractical for most people.
What is better than Excel for tracking expenses?
A mobile expense tracker app is better for daily logging because it's in your pocket at the moment you spend. The best option combines app convenience with spreadsheet-level data ownership — for example, Spentt logs in 5 seconds on your phone and stores your data in your own Google Drive, which you can export to Excel anytime.
Can I use Google Sheets as an expense tracker?
Yes — Google Sheets is more accessible than desktop Excel because it works on mobile. The Google Sheets mobile app is functional but still more friction than a dedicated expense tracker. Entering a new row, selecting a cell, typing the date, amount, and category takes 30-60 seconds per transaction versus 5 seconds in a purpose-built app. Over 365 days, that friction difference matters significantly for consistency.
How do I transfer my Excel expense data to an app?
Most expense tracker apps don't support CSV import from Excel — you'd typically start fresh. If continuity matters, consider keeping your historical Excel data as an archive and starting new logging in an app. Going forward, you can export from most apps (including Spentt) back to CSV/Excel for analysis at any time.
Is pen and paper still a valid way to track expenses?
Pen and paper is valid and underrated — especially for people who carry a notebook everywhere. The act of writing by hand creates stronger memory encoding than typing. The limitation is the same as Excel: you need the notebook with you at all times, and reviewing or analyzing the data requires manual work. For most people in the smartphone era, a mobile app provides equivalent immediacy with much better analysis.
