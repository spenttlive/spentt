Why small daily habits cost so much

A ₹180 coffee feels insignificant. It's less than 0.01% of a monthly salary for most people. But habits compound just like investments — in the opposite direction. The same daily ₹180 becomes ₹65,700 per year. Over 10 years, that's ₹6.57 lakh spent on coffee — and if that money had been invested at 12% SIP returns, it would be worth over ₹12 lakh.

This isn't an argument to never spend on things you enjoy. It's an argument to spend consciously — knowing the true cost of a habit before you decide whether it's worth it to you.

The latte factor — does it actually work?

The "latte factor" concept — popularised by David Bach — suggests that cutting small daily expenses and investing the savings leads to significant wealth over time. Critics argue that small cuts don't matter as much as earning more or making big financial decisions correctly.

Both are right. Cutting your daily coffee won't make you rich if you're not investing and growing your income. But awareness of the true cost of habits — which this calculator provides — changes how you evaluate spending decisions, which does change financial outcomes over time.

The question isn't "should I cut this habit?" The question is: "knowing the real cost, do I still think this is worth it?" That's a better question, and only you can answer it.

Frequently asked questions

How is the investment value calculated?
The investment value assumes you invest the daily habit cost as a monthly SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) at 12% annual returns, compounded monthly. This is a reasonable long-term average for Indian equity mutual funds. The actual return will vary based on market performance.
Should I actually quit my daily coffee to save money?
That depends on how much value it adds to your life. A ₹180 coffee that genuinely improves your morning, productivity, or enjoyment might be worth more than the ₹65,700/year it costs. The point of this calculator isn't to make you feel guilty — it's to make the decision conscious. Know the cost, then decide if it's worth it.
What's the most expensive common daily habit?
Among common Indian habits, smoking is typically the most expensive — a pack-a-day habit at ₹250/pack costs ₹91,250/year. Daily food delivery (₹450/day) costs ₹1,64,250/year. Daily Ola/Uber instead of metro (₹200/day) costs ₹73,000/year. These numbers often shock people who see them for the first time.
